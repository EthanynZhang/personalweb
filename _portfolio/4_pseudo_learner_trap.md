The Pseudo-Learner Trap investigates a central risk in AI-supported teacher education: an AI-simulated student may sound fluent, emotional, and learner-like without representing credible learning processes. The project compares expert assessments with pre-service teachers' judgments of simulated learner responses, focusing on error persistence, feedback uptake, short-term progression, and training suitability. It asks how validation evidence can help teachers distinguish surface authenticity from pedagogical validity and develops a Shared Validation Framework for the accountable use of AI-simulated students.
